# RetroBeam — Privacy, GDPR & Works Council Playbook

Status: draft v1 · 2026-07-17 · research verified July 2026. This is engineering guidance plus a rollout playbook, **not legal advice** — have the DPO/legal review before launch.

## 1. Why this document exists

RetroBeam is an employee-feedback tool for a German company. Two legal frames apply beyond ordinary GDPR hygiene:

- **§87(1) Nr. 6 BetrVG (works council co-determination):** applies to any technical system _objectively capable_ of monitoring employee behavior or performance — capability alone suffices, no monitoring intent needed (settled BAG case law). A tool with live presence, per-person notes, timestamps and vote logs is squarely capable. **Consequence: involve the Betriebsrat before rollout** and target a works agreement (Betriebsvereinbarung). Plan this during development (M1), not at launch. Once introduced with consent, later content changes (new templates etc.) are generally not separately co-determined.
- **GDPR legal basis:** for a voluntarily used team tool, §26 BDSG / Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest + genuine voluntariness + works agreement). Employee _consent_ is problematic in employment contexts due to power imbalance — don't build the compliance story on it.

## 2. Privacy by design — decisions already in the architecture

| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| No accounts; join via link + self-chosen display name (pseudonyms fine)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Minimal personal data; no e-mail addresses, no directory                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Private write phase enforced **server-side** (others' notes never sent over the wire)                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Anti-surveillance by design — a selling point, frame it that way                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Private write phase: **nobody** sees a foreign note before the reveal, the facilitator included                                                                                                                                                                                                           | The oldest rule in the product, and the one people assume wrong                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Presenting round: the facilitator DOES see more than the team.** Once the writing phase ends, a participant is handed a person's cards when the rotation puts that person on stage; the facilitator holds the whole board from the start, so they can moderate the round and see what is still to come. | **A deliberate asymmetry: during the presenting round the moderator sees content the room has not read yet — including its authorship on a non-anonymous board.** It never reaches the write phase. Separately, facilitator-hidden columns remain hidden from members in every phase. Say both facts plainly in a works-council conversation rather than claiming that the facilitator always sees exactly what the team sees. |
| Ghost cards carry no content and no true length signal                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Activity visible, behavior not measurable                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |

## 3. Data inventory (what exists, where, how long)

| Data                                         | Where                           | Lifetime                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Display name, assigned color, role           | Board DO (EU)                   | Until board deletion (≤90 days default)                                                               |
| Notes, reactions, votes, action items, kudos | Board DO (EU)                   | Same                                                                                                  |
| Session token                                | Participant's localStorage      | Local only                                                                                            |
| GIF search terms                             | Worker (transit only, proxied)  | Not stored; never reaches KLIPY with user IP                                                          |
| GIF media loads                              | KLIPY CDN (US)                  | Viewer IPs visible to CDN — see §4                                                                    |
| Application request logs                     | Disabled by design              | —                                                                                                     |
| Explicit error logs                          | Cloudflare Workers Logs         | Platform retention; errors/stacks and rejected GIF hostnames, never intentional board-content logging |
| Cloudflare invocation logs                   | Disabled (`observability.logs`) | —                                                                                                     |

Cloudflare's invocation logging is switched off in `apps/worker/wrangler.jsonc`
because it retains the full request URL for several days, and a board URL **is**
the participant capability (`/api/boards/:id`, `/ws`, `/export`) — retaining it
would both weaken the capability model and log GIF search terms we otherwise
never store. Explicit `console.error` output (a failed alarm arming, for
instance) is still emitted and reaches Workers Logs. It can include an error
message and stack or the hostname of a rejected GIF URL; the application does
not intentionally log board content.

## 4. Third parties / sub-processors

- **Cloudflare, Inc. (US):** hosting. EU jurisdiction DOs pin board data to EU data centers, but: full network-level residency is an Enterprise product (TLS still terminates at the nearest edge), and Cloudflare remains subject to the US CLOUD Act regardless of storage location. **Name this openly** in the DPIA/works agreement; the compensating measures are the standard post-Schrems-II set — EU-US Data Privacy Framework/SCCs, plus the fact that the data is low-sensitivity, minimized and short-lived. Normal, defensible practice for internal tooling; hiding it would be worse than stating it.
- **Kikliko, Inc. / KLIPY (US):** GIF search. Their privacy policy states they collect IP addresses and search history from API requests. Mitigations shipped: all search calls proxied through our Worker (KLIPY sees only the Worker's egress IP), no `customer_id` ever sent (it is optional on search), `content_filter=g` forced server-side. **Also pin the content filter on the API key itself in the KLIPY Partner Panel** — that one cannot be widened by any client, and a self-hoster brings their own key and so their own account configuration. Residual: GIF _media_ loads from their CDN expose viewer IPs — disclosed in the privacy notice. Disabling GIFs prevents new searches and GIFs, but existing GIFs keep loading until removed; R2 media caching is the v2 hardening if the works council asks.
- **Nothing else.** Emoji glyphs come from the user's device; no fonts, scripts, stylesheets or analytics load from third parties.

## 5. Rollout checklist

1. During M1: informal Betriebsrat heads-up + demo of the private write phase and this document.
2. Draft works agreement covering: purpose (team retrospectives, no performance evaluation), data inventory (§3), retention (90 days by default, with the facilitator's keep/delete controls), voluntariness, the available anonymous kudos/ROTI and blind voting, sub-processors (§4).
3. One-page **Löschkonzept**: default 90-day auto-delete, facilitator delete-now, export-then-purge. The app creates no backups; document Cloudflare's platform-side Durable Object recovery history separately.
4. In-app privacy notice (DE/EN) — **built 2026-09-11** at `/datenschutz` (`apps/web/src/content/privacy.*.tsx`), linked from the footer of every screen incl. the join gate: what's stored, where, how long, sub-processors, the GIF-CDN caveat. Lawyer review notes in `07-privacy-notice-review-notes.md`.
5. DPO review of DPIA-lite + this doc.
6. Launch note to teams: emphasize "your drafts never leave your browser's session until you submit; nobody — including admins — can read others' notes before reveal."
